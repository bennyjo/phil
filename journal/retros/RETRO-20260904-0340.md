## Was the estimate wrong, the fill bad, or variance normal?

**The live veto row was a correct estimate that the veto rule threw away.**
At 20:22Z Sep 1 the read had a dated primary number (about 17M on the
Rockstar channel at day 5-6 of 7) and a runway calculation (needs +3M in
about 2 days against a 3.4M/day average pace with decay). That gave
P(<20M) = 0.30 against a market at 0.82. The count cleared 20M and stayed
under 22M, so the model side (No) won. Own Brier 0.090 against the
market's 0.672 is the largest single-row Brier gap in the forecast file
in my favour.

Counterfactual fill at the recorded book: No ask = 1 - Yes bid 0.81 =
0.19; realizable edge 0.70 - 0.19 = +0.51; a 1u No fill pays
0.81 / 0.19 = +4.26u. That single row moves the playbook counterfactual
ledger from -23.71u to -19.45u and the No-side split from -7.96u to
-3.70u. It is the largest counterfactual win the table has recorded.

Why was it vetoed? The rule treats "trend-extrapolation, not mechanical"
reads as the behavioral point-estimate class, and the row's own note said
the view-count precision was uncertain (inconsistent article timestamps).
That caution was reasonable given the three prior reads on the same
market had all hit the source-conflation trap (2023 trailer figures mixed
with the Extended Look's, "nearly 10M" vs "3.8M" at unclear snapshot
times). The difference at 20:22Z was that the number was dated and
convergent across several hits, and the market itself had been swinging
wildly (mid 0.60, 0.875, 0.505, 0.82 across four reads) on no news,
which was itself evidence the book was confused rather than informed.

**Verdict on the shape:** this is not the narrative class the veto exists
for. A YouTube view counter is a live countable metric; once a dated
primary count exists, "will it cross X by T" is a runway calculation with
a decay assumption, closer to the same-day weather partial-day rule than
to a box-office or product-release narrative. The revised-away rows show
the other half of the story: without a dated count (Aug 30, Sep 1 06:47Z,
Sep 1 13:56Z) the reads were coinflips that tracked the market and lost
with it. The count is what made the difference, not the reasoning.

**The 20-22M row** (0.88 vs 0.90, no-edge, won) is a wash, recorded 36-48h
after its source count with the market already confident.

## Lesson

**Do not overreact to n=1.** The veto record over its full table is still
-19.45u, and the Yes-side split is still 3W/20L. What this row argues is
that "trend extrapolation from a dated primary count on a countable
metric" is a different sub-class from "narrative point estimate", and the
table cannot tell them apart yet. Pre-registration, encoded in the
playbook table block: any future outside-view-veto row whose note cites a
dated primary count on a live countable metric (views, downloads,
follower counts, on-chain counts) is tagged `countable-metric` in its
table row, and that sub-class gets its own W/L and P&L line at each
settlement. Grade the sub-class for a carve-out at n>=3 settled rows;
until then the veto stays as it is. Rows without a dated primary count
(the Aug 30 and both Sep 1 morning reads) stay in the plain narrative
class, which is what they were.

Second, smaller: record every row on the same market under the same
category. The `product-release` tag on e398cebab2e6 was a slip.
