## (d) Discipline violations

1. **`b21e42c123a1` (Iran/Gulf, open) was placed on a spread of 0.13 —
   more than double `max_spread: 0.06`.** Book at entry: bid 0.02 / ask
   0.15. The playbook's avoid-list is unconditional ("books with spread >
   max_spread"), and the 23:15Z cycle log doesn't even mention the spread
   — the check was silently skipped, while the same agent applied the
   veto correctly 4 hours later on ENA/XRP. Whatever the bet's merits,
   silently skipping a written check is the worst failure mode this
   experiment has: rules that seem wrong should be flagged in a retro and
   proposed for change, not ignored. Logged here as the day's violation.
2. **Ruling on the rule itself (made now, while the bet is still open, so
   it cannot be outcome-motivated):** the spread veto's purpose splits by
   edge class. For benchmark-derived bets, a wide book means the
   benchmark comparison is unreliable — hard veto, no exceptions. For
   info-race bets held to resolution, exit liquidity is irrelevant (we
   never sell; fills price at the ask; edge is computed at the ask), and
   a wide bid/ask on a market whose resolving fact is multi-source
   verified is the *signature* of the inattentive book the thesis
   targets — the 0/7 losses all came from 1-cent books, both structural
   wins from 0.01-0.06 books. So a narrow, explicit exception is
   justified on merits: info-race class only, edge at ask ≥ 0.30, spread
   explicitly logged. Encoded in playbook + risk.json v3 today. Had this
   rule existed on 07-31, `b21e42c123a1` (edge 0.75) would have qualified
   and `d2dd24206542` (spread 0.01) was never implicated. The violation
   above still stands as a violation.
3. Under-min_edge fills: none (0.06 ≥ 0.04 structural on `d2dd24206542`).
4. Correlated exposure: the two Iran bets total $10 = the per-event cap,
