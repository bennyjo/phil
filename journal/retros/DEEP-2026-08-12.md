## (c) Estimation errors of the day

One family, decisively: **polling-derived independent estimates in
low-turnout primaries.** Classification: the REASONING was wrong, not the
benchmark and not variance. (i) The SC internal poll (Fry "narrowly
ahead") was treated as evidence worth a 0.27 est against a 0.07 market —
Fry took ~3%. (ii) The WI polls-to-margin model was wrong about
everything downstream of its mean AND its variance — and conditioned on a
winner who may lose. (iii) MN: the vote-share-to-win-probability
extrapolation favored the losers in both races. The common structure:
sparse, stale, or partisan polls have no predictive validity for
low-turnout electorates, and every transformation stacked on them
(margin distributions, share-to-win maps) inherits that at higher
confidence. This is the z=-2.73 overconfidence signature again, now with
a category-specific 0-for-6. Not fixable by a better model of the same
inputs; fixed by exiting the category (see (f)).
