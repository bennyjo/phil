### The real pattern: edge class, not category

Sort the 9 settled bets by *why* we thought we had edge:

1. **Structural edges — 2/2 won.**
   - `2dc417ed68f6` (NG Up, est 0.97): an information race — the settlement
     input was already public, market hadn't finished repricing.
   - `1e8dec1078ba` (Paranaense No, est 0.78): a sibling-leg pricing
     inconsistency within one match's 1X2 complex.
2. **"My devig of public bookmaker odds beats a tight PM book" — 0/7 lost.**
   All seven (`1436bb727464`, `8e67cf4882bc`, `83ef29ef9493`,
   `4d5a4304a4d0`, `3cce11272d9d`, `1399450675ba`, `2c4c6a2adc0a`) were
   liquid sports markets with 1-cent spreads, where the claimed 0.04-0.08
   edge came from proportionally devigging scraped bookmaker odds and
   buying the side (always the cheaper side, prices 0.34-0.52) the devig
   favored.

P(0/7 | our estimates) ≈ 0.8%; P(0/7 | market prices) ≈ 1.7%. So this is
*partly* brutal variance — but there are two named, mechanistic process
flaws that plausibly account for the systematic ~4-6 cent gap:

- **Proportional devig inflates longshots.** Dividing raw implied
  probabilities by their sum spreads the vig evenly, but bookmakers load
  vig disproportionately onto longshots (favorite-longshot bias). Running
  the new `devig.py` on the actual entry odds: power devig prices the
  bought side ~0.5-1.5 cents lower than proportional at these 5-7%
  overrounds (e.g. Sun ML +144: proportional 0.390 → power 0.381). That
  alone eats a quarter-to-a-third of a 0.04 edge — real but not the whole
  gap; it compounds with benchmark staleness (see (c)) and the sharp
  counterparty below. Every one of the 7 losses bought the sub-0.55 side
  off a proportional devig.
- **A 1-cent-spread PM book is a sharp counterparty.** Someone quoting
  1-cent spreads with 24k depth on a WNBA game is pricing off the same
  bookmaker feeds with a proper devig, live. Betting into that book on
  devig arithmetic alone is a head-to-head contest with a better-tooled
  market maker — exactly the "sharp market" the playbook thesis says to
  avoid. Both wins came where that counterparty was absent or slow.

Honest caveat: n=7 cannot *prove* these mechanisms; the 0/7 could still be
~1% bad luck on a sound process, and the measured devig bias (~1 cent)
explains only part of the ~4-6 cent estimate-vs-market gap — the rest is
some mix of benchmark staleness, the sharp-counterparty prior, and plain
variance we cannot decompose at this n. The proportionate response is not
to stop betting sports but to **demand more edge from this edge class**
(0.07: base 0.04 plus a buffer covering devig bias + staleness risk) and
fix the devig method — done below.
