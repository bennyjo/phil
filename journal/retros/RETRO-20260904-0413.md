## Was the estimate wrong, the fill bad, or variance normal?

**The estimate got MORE wrong as the window closed, then recovered sharply
at the last check — a live lesson in trusting a real anchor over
trend-extrapolation.** Four sequential snapshots on the same `<20M`
market, tracing the actual view count as it climbed toward 20M:

- **b5c5c134d7cb (Aug30, no clean count):** est 0.55 built on no reliable
  live figure (source-conflation trap — conflicting 3.8M/5M+/10M reads).
  Landed closest to the eventual mid-ask consensus by luck, not method —
  brier 0.3025 is unremarkable, but the reasoning here amounted to a
  coin-flip with a fig-leaf of numbers behind it.
- **b3fbd3c3eef7 (Sep1 06:47Z):** est jumped to 0.85 (Yes, i.e. <20M)
  *matching* the market's own re-pricing to 0.875 with no new information
  found — this is the market-agrees skip working as intended, but the
  note itself flagged "convergence toward the low-view read, same pattern
  as the Mythos Sep1 leg" without noticing it was drifting the WRONG
  direction (actual views kept climbing toward 20M+, not staying under
  it). Brier 0.7225, the worst of the batch.
- **944d8e5fc4d0 (Sep1 13:56Z):** book collapsed to a coinflip (0.51) on
  its own; own est matched at 0.50, correctly read as no-edge. Neutral,
  uninformative row.
- **e398cebab2e6 (Sep1 20:22Z), the sole row with a real anchor:**
  WebSearch found a genuine dated view count (~17M as of Sep1, ~day 5-6 of
  7, ~3.4M/day pace) and extrapolated correctly to "very likely clears
  20M" — est 0.30 (Yes, i.e. <20M), the right side and, in hindsight, the
  right ballpark (brier 0.09, best of the batch by a wide margin).
  Outside-view-veto still fired (edge 0.51-0.52 on a trend-extrapolation,
  not a mechanical read) and correctly avoided a bet that would have won
  small — see counterfactual ledger update below.
- **e441fa8f0f8a (Sep3 12:52Z, sibling 20-22M bracket):** by this point
  the market itself had a live countable metric (~17.7M, 36-48h stale)
  and was confident (0.90); own est 0.88 tracked it almost exactly with
  no edge. This is the no-edge rule doing its job on a liquid,
  well-tracked market — nothing to fix.

**Lesson, concrete and new:** the two failures in this chain
(b5c5c134d7cb, b3fbd3c3eef7) both happened when WebSearch could not pin a
dated, sourced view count and the model either guessed or let the
market's own repricing serve as evidence in the wrong direction. The one
success (e398cebab2e6) is the only row in the whole family that had an
actual dated number with a computable pace. This confirms rather than
extends the standing rule (already in the counterfactual ledger's
"source-conflation trap" notes on this exact market): **a
trend-extrapolation forecast on a view-count market is only as good as
the freshest dated count it can find; absent one, no-edge/market-agrees
is the right call, not a guess dressed as an estimate.** No playbook rule
is new here — this is the same trap already documented on this market's
own siblings, now confirmed a third time on settlement. No edit.
