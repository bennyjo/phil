## Was the estimate wrong, or was this variance?

The point estimates (0.81, then 0.90 after the fresh-poll supersede) were
directionally right the whole time and converged toward the market as
more September polling came in (mean 32.5 -> 32.3 -> 33.8 across the
three records) — this reads as the poll-mean model tracking a real trend
(SPD strengthening through the final two weeks), not noise.

The two `outside-view-veto` rows are the ones with a table obligation.
`e056578ff5cf`'s own note is explicit about the mechanism: at the Sep 14
record, the point estimate (0.81, sd=1.7) matched the market's ask almost
exactly, but the wider sd band the veto rule requires checking (2.0-2.5)
pushed the implied No-side edge from 0.04 to 0.12-0.21 — past the 0.10
outside-view-veto boundary — so both sides were declined rather than
betting a point estimate the sd choice alone could flip. The market was
right (SPD cleared 31%), so the declined No-side counterfactual trade
lost. `52c0faf90ae2` is the same model applied to the adjacent 28-31%
bucket: center estimate ~0.24 against a market ask of 0.10, edge 0.14,
vetoed on the same sd-sensitivity shape, and it also lost because the
outcome landed one bucket over, in >=31%.

Read plainly: this is not evidence the veto rule is broken. The veto's
job is declining a bet whose sign or size depends on an unvalidated sd
choice, and the fact both declined sides happened to be on the losing
side of this particular settlement is exactly the kind of variance the
rule accepts in exchange for not eating the tail risk when the sd guess
is wrong instead of right. Two rows is not enough to move the boundary
(the relaxation fork below requires three consecutive walk-forward folds
plus other conditions, not a single pair of same-event siblings).
