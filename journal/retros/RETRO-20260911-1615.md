(headline MoM 0.36, core MoM 0.20/0.22, headline YoY 3.38, core YoY 2.38,
all read 2026-09-04, unchanged through two later rechecks), with dispersion

eligible and all correctly stayed forecast-only or vetoed.

Per bracket family, own-model probability mass assigned to the bracket that
actually won, vs. the market's:

- **Headline MoM (winner: 0.4%):** own 0.34 vs market 0.465 — market ahead.
- **Headline YoY (winner: 3.4%):** own 0.345 vs market 0.415 — market ahead.
- **Core YoY (winner: 2.4%):** own 0.376 vs market 0.425 — market ahead.
- **Core MoM (winner: 0.3%):** own 0.285 vs market ~0.29 — roughly tied,
  and on the adjacent 0.2% bucket (the one that did NOT happen) own model
  put only 0.38 vs the market's 0.516, i.e. narrowly better-calibrated on
  that leg specifically.

Three of four families: the market's tighter implied sd (~0.07) tracked
the print more accurately than the unsourced sd (0.10–0.11). The one
family where the own model matched or beat the market (core MoM) did so
via a *directional mean shift* the rationale flagged explicitly — "market
ladder skews up," own mean 0.22 vs the raw nowcast's 0.20 — not via the sd
choice. So this settlement cleanly separates two different moves inside
the same model: shifting the mean off the raw nowcast toward the market's
ladder shape held up; substituting an unsourced sd for the market's implied
one did not, on 3 of 4 independent tests.

This is now the fifth Gate-2-relevant settlement (after UMich 0/2, NFP
1W ambiguous, UR 0/1, BoI 0/0-but-correctly-excluded) and it points the
same direction: an unsourced dispersion input underperforms the market's
own implied spread more often than not. Encoded below.
