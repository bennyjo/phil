## Grading

The family splits cleanly by date. The Sep 21-23 rows lost to the market
on every row, and two of them by a lot. The Sep 30 to Oct 3 rows beat the
market on five of six graded rows; the sixth (`e6afa32bb249`) was a small
loss at the 0.84-vs-0.88 level.

Two method errors explain the early rows:

1. **Raw versus valid shares** (`4e52c227a60f`, 0.45). This is the
   pre-registered grade from the 2026-09-30 20:1xZ playbook entry: the
   raw-share row and the valid-vote row sat on opposite sides of the 0.88
   mid. The outcome was Yes. The converted row scored 0.0256 and the raw
   row 0.3025, so the conversion rule is confirmed. The market still beat
   both.
2. **Incoherent siblings.** On Sep 21 `1d98458aef28` put Lula most votes at
   0.65. Two days later `b350adc7e95c` put Lula 2nd at 0.15, which implies
   Lula 1st at about 0.85 in a two-horse race. Nothing in the polls moved
   20 points between those reads. Each leg was estimated on its own, so the
   family had no shared distribution, and the leg that drifted furthest
   (0.15) scored the worst Brier in the batch (0.7225). The late family
   (Oct 3: Lula 2nd 0.45, Lula most votes 0.55, Lula by <5 0.42, Flávio
   >=39 0.96) came from one stated margin distribution and is coherent:
   0.45 + 0.55 = 1, and P(Lula by <5) 0.42 < P(Lula 1st) 0.55.

The late rows shaded toward Flávio against the book, and the count went
further than the shading. Brazilian first-round polls have a pro-Lula
error history, and the notes already cite it. That is one event, so it
does not justify a rule.

implies a different distribution must supersede the older siblings in the
same cycle, or the note must say what changed.
