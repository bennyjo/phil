## The two bets

### NFP 0-50k No (84167af841f7) — WON +$2.46

Own model: mean 42k (pulled down from the Aug23 82.5k read by the ADP
miss, claims jump, and August seasonal pattern), sd 72k (sourced from
investinglive's August first-print consensus-miss history) → P(0-50k)=
0.27, No @ ask 0.67, edge 0.06.

**Right conclusion, wrong reasoning — this needs to be said plainly.** The
bet won because the actual print landed in the "at least 150k" bracket
(the model's own P(>=150k) was 6.5%, the market's was 6%), not because
the wider-sd dispersion thesis anticipated *this* outcome. The Aug23→Sep4
revision moved the mean DOWN on ADP-miss evidence; the actual surprise was
a massive beat in the opposite direction. A No bet on "0-50k" wins against
almost any print outside a fairly narrow window, so this specific bet's
win says little about whether the mean-shift reasoning was sound — it
wasn't, directionally. What it does say: **both the model and the market
underpriced NFP tail risk this print** (market's own >=150k bracket ask
was 6-7c; ours matched it almost exactly), which is the more useful
finding than "the dispersion thesis worked."

### UR 4.1% No (609c98073a77) — LOST -$5.00

Own model: mean 4.11, sd 0.12 (BLS technical-note CI scaled + empirical
calm-period read, consensus-hit-rate ~1/3 over 2024-26) → P(4.1)=0.34,
No @ ask 0.61, edge 0.05. Market's implied sd was tighter (~0.09, pricing
the 4.1 bracket at 0.405). Actual: exactly 4.1%. **The market's tighter
dispersion was right this time; ours was too wide.** Estimate wrong, not
fill or variance-noise: the whole edge was the sd disagreement, and the
sd disagreement resolved against us cleanly (this is the single point
that decides win/loss on an exact-bracket market — there's no partial
credit).

### The dispersion-pair thesis, graded

Pre-registered framing: "markets price the consensus bracket too tight."
Mixed at n=2, not confirmable either way: NFP's outcome is consistent with
"the market is too tight around consensus" in the broad sense (both of us
gave the actual outcome ~6-7%, i.e. both were surprised, but our wider sd
gave marginally more room for it than a naive market read of ~60k sd
would). UR's outcome directly contradicts it (market's tighter sd nailed
the print, ours didn't). Two same-day econ-jobs "other"-class bets,
1W ($+2.46) / 1L (-$5.00), net -$2.54 — score.py's `other` edge class is
now n=5, win=0.40 (2W/3L), brier_delta +0.0579 after this settlement
(PPI won, WTI lost, AfD still open and excluded, plus these two). Small-n
rule applies (mind small n below ~15); no floor or policy change from
n=2. Filed as the first two data points on standard-floor (sub-0.10 edge,
not carve-out) self-modeled econ-jobs dispersion bets — worth revisiting
once a few more of this exact shape settle.

**Concrete lesson, encoded in playbook.md (see below):** a self-modeled
sd for headline NFP needs to budget more probability for genuine outlier
prints than a Gaussian-with-sourced-sd naturally gives — both sides of
this book gave the actual outcome ~6-7%, and it happened. This doesn't
change any floor (the bet that would have needed a different sd, the
0-50k No, won anyway), but it's a live flag against reading the NFP win
as validation of the sd-widening method, and against tightening UR's sd
on this one data point alone (n=1, exact-bracket markets are inherently
high-variance to grade on a single settlement).
