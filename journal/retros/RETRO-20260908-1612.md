## What actually happened

Checked, rather than assumed, because the failure mode had two very
different candidate explanations and only one of them is fixable.

The FC Utrecht v Go Ahead Eagles Eredivisie match was suspended
2026-09-06 at 1-3 (Go Ahead leading) after 64 minutes when Utrecht fans
threw fireworks. The forecast note claimed the KNVB had officially
ordered the final ~26 minutes replayed behind closed doors on Tuesday
2026-09-08 at 14:00 CEST (12:00Z). **That read was correct.** The KNVB
competition committee did order the resumption without spectators after
consulting the municipality, and the match was resumed at exactly that
time at Stadion Galgenwaard. Go Ahead Eagles then conceded twice in the
remaining ~26 minutes and the match finished **3-3**. Go Ahead did not
win, the market resolved No, and the loss is a straightforward football
outcome.

Worth stating plainly because it is the opposite of the last comparable
self-catch: this was **not** a resolution-mechanism error. The
distinction matters. The 2026-09-07 Iran self-catch (a7795e4) was a
clause-to-outcome mapping failure - reading the question text instead of
the resolution criteria. Here the resolution criteria, the resumption
ruling, the venue, the date and the kickoff time were all researched
correctly and all held. The estimate of the football is what lost.

## Finding 1: the estimate loss is variance, not a defect

Tempting to write a rule here. The evidence says do not.

- The forecast stream's 0.9-1.0 calibration bucket is **n=36, realized
  0.89**. A bucket that well calibrated is *expected* to produce about
  four losses in 36 rows. This is one of them.
- The sourced base rate was real: teams that gain a two-goal lead win
  ~89.6-90% (Sky Sports analysis of PL data since 1992, corroborated by
  a second analysis). With the lead already banked and only 26 minutes
  left, sitting at or slightly above that base rate is defensible.
- n=1 in a category at n=9 settled (`market-microstructure`
  brier_delta +0.0750 before this row), far under the ~15-settlement bar
  CYCLE.md sets for a category verdict.

One thing is worth **pre-registering rather than legislating**, because
at n=1 it cannot be distinguished from the variance above. The note
identified a structural novelty in its own words - a standalone
26-minute resumption after a two-day gap, behind closed doors, with both
sides rested and the home crowd removed, is not the same object as "26
minutes remaining in a continuous match" - called it "untested", and
then adjusted for it by roughly one point, landing at 0.91, *above* the
naive base rate. Meanwhile the market sat 0.15 below that base rate at a
0.227 spread. A book that wide is the market saying nobody knows how to
price this; the 0.15 discount to the base rate was plausibly the market
pricing the exact factor I had named and then not priced.

Pre-registered claim to grade later, not a rule today: **when I name an
unmodelled structural factor and the market sits materially below a base
rate I consider well sourced, the market's discount is evidence about
that factor's size, and a token adjustment in the opposite direction is
the wrong response.** This shape (confident self-built point estimate,
interpretive or novel situation, market better) is the same one the
`outside-view-veto` slice already carries at n=104 / +0.0430, so the
prior is not neutral - but this row is not veto-class and one row is not
evidence. Grade against the next three `market-microstructure` or
novel-structure rows where I depart from a base rate against the market.

## Finding 2: the skip reason is mislabelled, and it corrupts two instruments

This one is a process error, independent of the outcome, and it is
actionable now.

The recorded skip_reason is `no-edge`. The row's own note says otherwise,
in its own words: "Nominal ask-edge 0.041 clears min_edge (0.04) but the
spread (0.227) is far over max_spread (0.06) ... no bet, **spread veto
binds**." The edge cleared the floor. The operative reason for the
decline was `max_spread`. Under the DEEP-2026-08-14 taxonomy rule that
is `wide-spread-veto`, not `no-edge`.

The magnitude makes this worth acting on rather than noting:

- `no-edge` currently reads **n=307, brier_delta +0.0009** (aggregate
  ≈ +0.276). This single row contributes **+0.2573** of it. Excluding
  it, the remaining 306 rows sum to roughly +0.02 - a mean on the order
  of **+0.0001**, i.e. flat. Allowing for the printed rounding on
  +0.0009 the remaining mean lands somewhere in +0.00001 to +0.0001;
  the conclusion is robust either way. One mislabelled row is
  responsible for essentially the entire apparent drift in the repo's
  main clean-feed-null instrument.
- `wide-spread-veto` currently reads **n=4, brier_delta -0.0553**
  (agent better on every row, the basis for the DEEP-2026-08-20 "4/4
  agent-better" note). With this row it would be **n=5 at ≈ +0.0072** -
  the slice flips from uniformly agent-better to roughly neutral. That
  is the spread rule's first datapoint where the veto *saved* money
  rather than costing it, and it is currently invisible.

So the mislabel simultaneously makes the no-edge slice look like it has
drift it does not have, and hides the strongest single piece of evidence
in favour of the spread rule. That is exactly the "one label averaging
two mechanisms with opposite settled signs corrupts both instruments"
argument DEEP-2026-08-14 made - recurring a month later at a much larger
magnitude.

`journal/forecasts.jsonl` is written only by `core/forecast.py` and
`core/resolve.py`, so the row cannot be corrected in place and this
retro does not try to. The correction goes in the playbook as an
erratum, so future reads of both slices are right.
