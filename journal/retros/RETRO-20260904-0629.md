## Was the estimate wrong, the fill bad, or variance normal?

**Three of the four calls were directionally right and well-calibrated;
the fourth was a real miss.**

- **925eb1c697f9 / cfda85a4abde (same market, two snapshots):** both
  correctly read "available soon" ≠ a confirmed by-Sep3 date, holding
  est_prob(Yes) at 0.20 then 0.15 against asks of 0.88/0.90. Brier
  0.04/0.0225 — excellent. The fact-finality gate (DEEP-2026-08-30)
  declined to bet despite ~0.66-0.73 nominal No-side edge purely because
  the underlying claim was an unconfirmed timeline rumor, not because the
  read was wrong — and the read was right.
- **b1d2b955da88:** same shape, est 0.18 vs ask 0.849, Brier 0.0324. Also
  correctly read the Sep1 OpenAI blog post as confirming "available soon"
  with no committed date.
- **a8d12b4831b4, the one real miss:** by Sep3 17:32Z, with a teaser
  ("stars almost aligned"), API/Codex sightings, and a scheduled watch
  stream, the model moved to est 0.80 (Yes) — betting that same-day
  public launch was likely. The market had moved further still (ask
  0.948). Actual: No. Brier 0.64. The rationale at record time reasoned
  correctly that timing AND the public-access clause were both
  unconfirmed, and still leaned toward Yes on the strength of the teaser
  language — a case of narrative momentum (a live-launch-feeling day)
  outweighing the actual resolution criterion (general public access,
  not merely a teaser or a beta sighting). This is the same failure
  shape flagged elsewhere in the ledger for hype-adjacent product/release
  questions: a strong qualitative signal that a launch is *imminent* is
  not evidence about whether it clears a specific, narrow access
  threshold *by a specific date*.

**Lesson, concrete:** on public-access-gated release markets, teaser
language and component sightings (API strings, beta streams) should move
the estimate toward "launch is close" but not toward "the specific
narrow access clause resolves Yes today" — those are different claims
with very different confirmation bars, and this cycle's a8d12b4831b4 note
already distinguished them in prose ("the exact-day timing and the
public-access clause are unconfirmed") without letting that distinction
move the number enough. No playbook edit: this is a specific-instance
miss on a single volatile day, not a pattern seen elsewhere in the
`ai-model-release` category (three of four rows this tick were well
calibrated), and n=1 miss is not grounds for a new rule.
