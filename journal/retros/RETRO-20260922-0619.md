right for the wrong reason it later doubted; snapshot 2 was the miss.

Both snapshots 1 and 2 carried `outside-view-veto`, so no capital moved on
either — the veto did its job independent of which estimate was closer.
The counterfactual ledger confirms this mechanically: both declined

veto's job (block the bet) and the estimate's job (track the truth) can
be graded separately, and only the second one slipped.

**Lesson, already self-recorded in the snapshot-3 note and re-affirmed
here:** a price move with no sourced cause is not itself evidence — it is
exactly the "estimate distrust" condition `outside-view-veto` exists to
catch, which is why the veto (not a strategy edit) is what actually
protected this position. No playbook rule change: the existing veto
machinery already produced the correct outcome on the miss; the residual
error was in a forecast-only estimate, which forecast.py's own
supersede-and-grade mechanism is what's designed to correct, and it did
(snapshot 3 walked it back before settlement). Filing this as normal
variance in the `ai-model-release`/`product-release` forecast-calibration
